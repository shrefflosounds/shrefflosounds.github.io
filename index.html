Overview

What Does Sidehop Do?

An approachable, joyful video game creation studio built right into your web browser.

Have you ever wanted to build your own Mario-style video game, but found game engines too complex, confusing, or full of technical jargon? Sidehop is designed for you.

Sidehop turns your web browser into an open canvas. You can click and place ground tiles, slippery ice ramps, musical note blocks, floating platforms, springboards, spikes, and goal flags onto a clean grid. At any moment, pressing the Spacebar drops you straight into your level as the hero, letting you test your jumps and obstacles instantly.

Inside the built-in Art Studio, you can sketch your own custom characters and tiles with markers, brushes, and stamps—or simply tint any starter component (like turning an ice block into a fiery red gem or coloring your victory flag gold). When your level is ready, you can save it as a lightweight file or export a standalone HTML game that your friends and family can play on any computer, anywhere, without needing an internet connection.

Quickstart

How Should Someone Get Started?

Creating your first playable stage takes less than two minutes. Follow these four simple steps:

Step 1

Pick Your Pieces

Select any component from the bottom drawer—solid blocks, ice ramps, music note boxes, springs, or walkers—and click to place them on the grid.

Step 2

Paint & Recolor

Double-click any object to open the Art Studio. Paint your own visuals, draw silly faces with stamps, or pick a vibrant color tint for starter blocks.

Step 3

Press Space to Hop

Tap the Spacebar to jump into Play mode immediately! Move with Arrow keys or A/D, leap with W or Space, bounce on note boxes, and reach the goal flag.

Step 4

Save & Share

Click "Export Level" to save your .sidehop file, or export a standalone HTML game that your friends can play on any computer without installing anything.

Creator & Story

Who Made This, How & Why?

A college creative project exploring the joy of tactile, accessible digital making.

I created Sidehop for a college project centered on interactive media, visual expression, and making creative game tools accessible to everyone. Many modern game-making platforms come with steep learning curves, dense configuration menus, and complicated programming hurdles that discourage people from simply having fun. I wanted to create something that feels as immediate, friendly, and joyful as opening a fresh sketchbook and doodling with markers, while offering real interactive physics and audio feedback.

Open Source & Rights

Open Source Licensing & Respect for Terms

Honoring creators, software freedom, and standard open web technology.

Sidehop is fully committed to ethical, transparent open-source practices. It is built strictly on open web standards and honors all software licenses and creator attributions:

  • MIT Open Source License: Sidehop's core application code is released under the permissive MIT License. You are free to inspect the code, learn from its architecture, adapt it, and build upon it.
  • SIL Open Font License: All interface and display typography utilizes fonts released under the SIL Open Font License, respecting the craftsmanship and licensing of independent type designers.
  • Native Browser Web APIs: All rendering and sound generation are built directly on native browser standards—HTML5 2D Canvas for high-performance graphics, Web Audio API for synthetic sound effects and diatonic music notes, and MediaStream Recording API for video capture. No proprietary third-party analytics, locked SDKs, or cloud telemetry libraries are used.
  • Complete User Ownership: Everything you make in Sidehop belongs entirely to you. You hold 100% ownership over all levels, artwork, and exported games you create.

Sidehop stands on the shoulders of legendary creative game-making tools and responsive platformers:

Super Mario Maker (Nintendo, 2015) Game Flow & Sandbox UI

Directed by Takashi Tezuka and produced by Shigeru Miyamoto. Inspired Sidehop's seamless in-place playtesting, snap-to-grid entity placement, corner flyout component menus, mystery question blocks, springboards, note blocks, donut drop lifts, and flagpole victory celebrations.

Kid Pix & Kid Pix 3D (Broderbund, 1989/1994) Art Studio & Tactile Feel

Created by Craig Hickman. The guiding spirit behind Sidehop's Art Studio: chunky tactile toolbars, wacky stamps, squishy audio feedback, and the philosophy that creating things should be messy, joyful, and hilarious.

Drawn to Life (5th Cell / THQ, 2007) Personalization & Custom Heroes

Directed by Jeremiah Slaczka. Inspired Sidehop's player personalization: the magical feeling of drawing your own hero and platforms directly into a living physics engine.

WarioWare D.I.Y. (Nintendo, 2009) Micro-Creation & Tuning

Directed by Goro Abe. Inspired the Component Inspector: tactile parameter sliders making physics tuning (gravity, speed, bounciness) accessible and fun for all ages.

Sonic the Hedgehog & Celeste Kinetic Feel & 45° Slopes

Sonic Team (Yuji Naka & Hirokazu Yasuhara) for 45° incline/decline slope momentum and spring mechanics; Maddy Thorson & Noel Berry (Celeste) for coyote time, jump buffering, and snappy platforming feel.

Sidehop is built from scratch with zero heavy frameworks, running entirely on modern open web standards:

HTML5 Canvas 2D Context W3C / WHATWG Standard

Custom-engineered 60 FPS subpixel platformer engine. Features subpixel AABB collision detection, 45-degree slope heightmap physics, dynamic squash-and-stretch matrices, smooth camera lerp tracking, and procedural terrain tile rendering.

Web Audio API Synthesizer W3C Recommendation

Zero-asset procedural chiptune sound generator inspired by Tomas Pettersson's sfxr / bfxr algorithms. Utilizes native Web Audio API oscillators, exponential gain ramps, and procedural white-noise buffers without loading any external sound files.

MediaRecorder Video API W3C Recommendation

Direct 60 FPS canvas video capture pipeline utilizing the MediaStream Recording API to record crisp WebM/MP4 gameplay clips and trigger instant client-side downloads without any server encoding.

Standalone Single-File Web Export Open Architecture

Exports self-contained HTML files with embedded level data, custom painted visuals, and physics configs that run entirely offline in any modern web browser with zero external dependencies.
